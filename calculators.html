What can you calculate with CapLink?

The calculators are grouped around the questions people usually ask before a money decision.

"How much could my investments grow?"

The SIP calculator estimates the growth of a fixed monthly investment, while the Lumpsum calculator does the same for a one-time amount. Both use an expected rate of return that you choose, so try a few rates to see a realistic range.

"What will this loan really cost?"

The EMI calculator shows your monthly instalment, and the Loan calculator shows how much of the total goes to interest. Comparing tenures makes the trade-off between a smaller EMI and a larger interest bill easy to see.

"Is my money keeping up with prices?"

The Inflation calculator shows how costs may rise over time. Pair it with the FD calculator or the Gold calculator to see how savings and gold values compare with rising prices.

"Am I on track for later in life?"

The Retirement calculator estimates the corpus you may need, the SWP calculator shows how regular withdrawals could last, and the Tax calculator gives an estimate to help you plan the year ahead.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a financial calculator?

A financial calculator turns a few inputs — such as an amount, an interest rate, and a time period — into an estimate, like a monthly EMI or the possible future value of an investment. It helps you see how changing one number affects the result.

When should I use a SIP calculator?

Use a SIP calculator when you're thinking about investing a fixed amount every month. It estimates how much you'd invest in total and what the investment could grow to at an assumed rate of return, so you can compare different monthly amounts and time periods.

What does an EMI calculator help me understand?

An EMI calculator shows the monthly instalment for a loan and how much of the total you'd pay as interest. Comparing tenures helps you see the trade-off between a lower EMI and a higher total interest cost.

Why are calculator results only estimates?

Results depend on the inputs and assumptions you enter, such as interest rates, returns, and inflation. Real-world rates change, lenders add fees, and markets move up and down, so actual outcomes can differ from any calculation.
